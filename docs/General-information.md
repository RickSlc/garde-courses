


# General information {-}

## What can GARDE do? {-}

GARDE can be used to address epidemiologic, health services, cancer, genetic and other research questions.  It support a variety of study designs. Check out potential use cases that we put together [here](https://garde.utah.edu/potential-use-cases.html). If you have specific research ideas you want to discuss, please email [the GARDE study team](mailto:GARDE@hci.utah.edu).

## Who developed GARDE? {-}

GARDE was developed by an [established biomedical informatics research team]((https://reimagineehr.utah.edu/about/)) at University of Utah Health (UofUHealth) with funding from the National Cancer Institute ([U24CA204800](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/r6C7YNO7hkamZGNfiBrrFA/project-details/9979779)). The National Cancer Institute (NCI) supported a successful trial of GARDE at UofUHealth and New York University Langone Health ([U01CA232826](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03985852)). With NCI support ([U24CA274582]((https://reporter.nih.gov/search/40pDEPHmgE21hhMTAe0sRQ/project-details/10468229#similar-Projects)), GARDE is being implemented at Medical University of South Carolina and Weil Cornell Medicine.

## How much does GARDE cost? {-}

GARDE is open source and free. The GARDE study team is funded by NCI to support its implementation. GARDE implementation and maintenance requires facility-based support from information security, biomedical informatics, EHR subject matter expertise, and genetic counseling.  We estimate that technical implementation may require ~200 hours of support over the initial 8 months of use. (For imformation on implementing GARDE, see our [Implementation Overview](https://garde.utah.edu/garde-implementation-overview.html) or the information on [GARDE Governance Review](https://garde.utah.edu/garde-implementation.html)).

## Who should I talk to in my organization about using GARDE? {-}

We recommend engaging individuals from the following groups early in implementation: Informatics, Information security, clinical care (including genetic counseling), research facilitation, and information technology (including healthcare system governance committees).
